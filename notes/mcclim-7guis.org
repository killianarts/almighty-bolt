#+TITLE: Three 7GUIs apps in McCLIM: does its presentation layer take the wiring away

=notes/mcclim-stress-apps.org= found that McCLIM's own gadgets are as long and as wired as our
retained variant, and that the stress apps never used the layer that makes
=other-frameworks/task-board.lisp= nice: presentations, translators, commands with typed
arguments, =accept= and =updating-output=. Micah asked for the 7GUIs apps that have objects and
operations on them (the crud, the circle drawer, the flight booker) in McCLIM, so that the layer
is used. The apps already exist in the three variants, with tests, sizes and the change-cost
edits, so only the McCLIM side is built. This file says what is built, how it is counted and
what I expect, before any of it is written. Written 2026-10-27 on the branch =mcclim=, from
=98a5274=.

* What I settled first, in the scratchpad (McCLIM master =dcde4bc=, the null port)
Five mechanisms whose answers change what the apps can be, found by trying them (and, where
said, by reading the source) and not by recall.
- A =blank-area= translator is given the click's =x= and =y= (a click at 250 gave 249: the
  coordinates of the pane, one off in my injected event, which I take for my event and not
  for McCLIM). A click on a presentation that has no translator for the gesture falls through to the
  =blank-area= translator of the pane, so "a click on a circle draws nothing" needs a tester on
  the translator or a translator on the circle.
- A translator bound to =:gesture :menu= fires its command directly on a right click: there is no menu
  to choose from, which is what the circle drawer's secondary button wants. (A =blank-area=
  translator with =:menu t=, its default, would be in the menu that a right click on empty ground
  makes; so it needs =:menu nil=.)
- A =defstruct= is not a presentation type (a =defclass= is: =task-board.lisp= has none to
  define); every shared model here is a =defstruct=, so each type that is presented needs one
  =define-presentation-type=.
- An =:accept-values= pane in the layout of a frame keeps its fields on the screen next to the
  other panes (=Examples/av-dingus.lisp=): its display function calls =accept= and puts what it
  returns in slots, and =accept-values-command-button= makes a button. =accept= makes a gadget
  by the type: a =completion= is an option pane, a =boolean= a toggle button, a real with
  bounds a slider, a string a text field, and anything else (a type of mine) a text input editor
  that draws its text in another ink when it does not parse. (The mapping of types to gadgets is read
  from =compute-default-gadget-view=; what I saw running is the =member= as an option pane and the
  date field's box.)
- An =accepting-values :own-window t= dialog opens as a frame of its own on the null port, and
  can be closed from outside; run by the command in the frame's thread it blocks that thread,
  which is what a modal dialog is.

* What is built
One package and one file for each, in =apps/7guis/<task>/mcclim*.lisp=, in a system of its own,
=almighty-bolt-7guis/mcclim=, which =make test= and =make image= never load, and not registered
with the variants' registry. The models are shared as they are (=crud/model=, =flight/model=,
=circles/model=, including the undo of the circle drawer, which is the model's and not McCLIM's);
the circle painter pushes to our display list and cannot be, so the McCLIM circles draw
themselves.
- *Crud, with the layer* (=crud/mcclim.lisp=): the entries are presented, each as an object of type
  =entry=, in a pane whose display function uses =updating-output= keyed by the entry's id; a
  click is a translator to =com-pick=; the filter, the name and the surname are in an
  =:accept-values= pane, with Create, Update and Delete as command buttons; the state is in
  slots of the frame.
- *Crud, with gadgets* (=crud/mcclim-gadgets.lisp=): a =list-pane=, three =text-field= gadgets and
  three =push-button= gadgets, with callbacks. The same library in its other architecture, as the
  dashboard was, and the only twin: the other two apps would need a pane subclass with a
  =handle-event= method to be written with gadgets (a canvas of circles, a form that paints a field red), which the line below does not
  allow.
- *Flight booker, with the layer* (=flight/mcclim.lisp=): an =:accept-values= pane: the kind a
  =(member :one-way :return)=, the dates of a presentation type =flight-date= whose =accept=
  method calls the shared =parse-date= and whose =present= method calls =format-date=, a Book
  command button, and the message.
- *Circles, with the layer* (=circles/mcclim.lisp=): a pane whose display function presents each
  circle (type =circle=, drawn with =draw-circle*=) under =updating-output=; a =blank-area=
  translator to =com-draw= with the click's place; a translator on =circle= with =:gesture :menu=
  to =com-adjust=, which opens =accepting-values :own-window= with =(accept '(integer 5 200))=
  (a slider) and, when it is accepted, does one =drawing-resize=; =com-undo= and =com-redo= in a
  =:command-menu= pane.

* The line, as for the stress apps
Allowed: everything McCLIM exports and documents, used as documented: commands, translators,
presentation types, =accept=, =accepting-values=, =updating-output=, gadgets, drawing in a display
function. A presentation type for the dates, whose methods call the shared =parse-date=, is the
vocabulary of the app, which is what presentation types are for, and so is allowed; it is listed here and its
lines are counted in the app. Not allowed: a new gadget or pane class, a =handle-event= method, a
patch to McCLIM, an undo that is not the model's. =updating-output= is used where there is
something to keep (the crud's list and the circles), and not elsewhere. Where a part of an app cannot be
done with this, it stays undone and is said so.

* How it is counted
- Size by =scripts/mcclim-size.lisp= extended to the 7GUIs paths: lines, tokens (the whole file and
  after the first form), concepts (the symbols imported from the CLIM packages), with our
  variants' files next to them, and for the circles with =circles/paint.lisp= (36 lines)
  added to each variant's, because McCLIM draws in its own file.
- *Wiring* is the lines whose only job is to keep what is shown in step with the model: handles to
  widgets kept to be changed later; what sets a widget's text, selection, enabled state or look
  from the state outside the place where the widget is described; callbacks and handlers that
  only copy a value between a widget and the state; signals, components and subscriptions made
  to carry a change. Not counted: the model's operations and their calls, the layout, the
  predicates that say whether something is valid (called where the widget is described, they are
  the description), and what handles an event by changing the model. The same rule is applied by
  reading to the five McCLIM apps and to our nine files of these three tasks, and the lines
  counted are listed in the results. It is a reading, and the author of the apps reads them.
- Sizes and wiring are reported only next to the *spec map*: the acceptance tests of each task
  (=apps/7guis/t/acceptance/=: 10 for the crud, 9 for the flight booker, 13 for the circles),
  each marked met, met differently (said how) or not met, for each McCLIM version. A version that
  is shorter because it does not grey out Update is not a saving, so a size is compared only for the
  tests that both sides meet.
- McCLIM-side tests (lisp-unit2, McCLIM's null port, in a system of their own): that the display
  makes presentations of the right type and object, found by walking the output records; the
  commands called directly; that a translator applies, by McCLIM's own query for the applicable
  ones; the models' behaviour already has its tests. A click with an injected pointer event
  through the frame's loop on the null port, and once on XQuartz (an event injected into McCLIM's
  queue, not one from X: there is no tool here to click an X window) with a screenshot, for each app. The
  dialog's body is tested apart if the own-window dialog cannot be run headless.
- No cost is measured: these are not stress apps.

* What I expect
Written before the apps, so that it can be wrong.
** The spec map
| task    | version | met | met differently | not met |
|---------+---------+-----+-----------------+---------|
| crud    | gadgets |  10 |               0 |       0 |
| crud    | layer   |   8 |               2 |       0 |
| flight  | layer   |   5 |               4 |       0 |
| circles | layer   |   7 |               3 |       3 |
- Crud with the layer, differently: Update, Delete and Create are not on the screen when they could not be
  pressed (a command button that is not displayed, and not one that is grey): 2 tests.
- Flight: the return field is not shown for a one-way flight (and not greyed), Book is not
  shown when the flight is invalid (4 tests among those about what cannot be used); "an invalid date
  looks different" is met by McCLIM's own ink, which may not be red; "changing anything clears the
  message" is met only with a few lines that notice the change.
- Circles, differently: the circle under the pointer is not grey but is highlighted by McCLIM's own
  presentation highlight; the dialog is a window of its own and modal, with OK and Cancel, and not a
  scrim over the canvas. Not met: the diameter does not change live behind the dialog (the dialog is
  modal and nothing redisplays under it), clicking away does not close the dialog and keep the change,
  and "the canvas takes no clicks while the dialog is open" is met only if a click on the other window is
  not queued (it may be handled after the dialog closes; if it is, it is not met).
** Size and wiring
- Crud with the layer is shorter than the immediate crud (77 lines) by 15 to 30 %, with 4 to 6 lines of
  wiring (the pick copies the entry into the two fields, and the filter's change must redisplay the list); the gadget version is 85 to 100
  lines, like the retained crud (97), with 14 to 18 lines of wiring (setting the list's items, the
  fields, the buttons' =gadget-active-p=).
- Flight with the layer: 40 to 50 lines with the date type (about 25 without it), against 54 to
  62 of ours: the parsing, the choice and the invalid look are McCLIM's; 2 to 4 lines of wiring (the message).
- Circles with the layer: 60 to 80 lines, against 87 (immediate) plus 36 (the painter): 35 to 50 % shorter,
  and it is the dialog (=accept= of a bounded integer is a slider) and the translators that make
  it so; 3 to 6 lines of wiring (the command menu's enabled state after each change).
- So: with the layer the wiring is about a quarter of the gadget version's or less, and the apps are
  shorter than our immediate variant's by what McCLIM does for them (parse, validate, choose, draw
  a dialog), and they meet less of the spec, by the table above. What would show me wrong: the
  layer's crud no shorter than our immediate crud, its wiring over half of the gadget version's, or the
  spec met in fewer than the table says by a wide margin (then the layer is not as ready as I think).

* The edits
The change-cost edits E1 (an Email field in the crud), E2 (an optional return date), E4 (hide
the filter) and E5 (a dialog in two columns) are aimed at these three apps, and applying them to the
McCLIM versions would be the change-cost test of the layer. That is not part of this; it is offered.
