#+TITLE: 0023 — Instrumentation and metrics: phases, settling, images, idle, memory
#+DATE: 2026-10-26
- Status :: proposed

* Context
Step 1 of Phase 3. The harness of 0021 timed only the variant's build, in a loop
with no renderer. The charter asks for more: the frame split into phases, each
with its time, garbage and bytes; the cost of settling shown apart; a trace to
look at single frames; and the metrics of an app on a machine: startup, idle CPU
and wakeups, memory, image size and compile time, with the idle rule (no variant
may use CPU when idle) as a floor. Two things had to be found out first: whether
an executable can be saved at all with AppKit and Metal in it (startup and image
size are of a saved executable), and whether a callback written in Lisp can
return a struct (the IME's firstRectForCharacterRange: returns an NSRect, and a
no for that would have meant a C shim).

* What was built
- Saved executables. The platform layer could not be saved: the frameworks were
  loaded with =:dont-save=, and every class and selector was a load-time value
  of the process that saved the image. A literal class or selector is now a cell
  that is filled in when the code is loaded and again by =reinitialize-objc= (an
  init hook); =define-runtime-class= and =define-startup= make the three
  run-time classes and their ivar offsets again. A test saves a small image in a
  process of its own and runs it; without the hook it errors. =make image
  VARIANT=immediate= saves =build/immediate= with the seven tasks in it; the
  task is chosen by =TASK=.
- The context carries the instrumentation, off or on (=context-phases-p=).
  =with-phase= adds the real time (not the core clock, which replay makes
  virtual) of a variant's update, layout or paint to a slot of the context, and
  =settle-frame= times its first build and all of them. The host clears the
  slots at the start of a frame and, with the instrumentation on, puts them in
  the ring, with the garbage collected and the GPU's time for the frame before.
  A ring record is 16 words now. The three variants mark their phases: update is
  what a retained variant's tickers do, what a reactive one's signals and
  reconciler do, and calling the interface function of an immediate one.
- =write-trace= writes the ring as Chrome trace JSON (Perfetto reads it;
  Python's JSON parser reads it in the test), and =summarize-ring= gives the
  frame and its parts, the builds, the cost of settling (the time of all the
  variant's builds less that of the first) and the garbage.
- =replay= can make its frames through a function, so that the host's
  =run-frame= makes them, with the real renderer, into an off-screen texture.
  =make bench-run= does both kinds of run, headless (as before) and host, with
  the variants interleaved in the host runs: one replay in each in turn, ten
  rounds, because the CPU's and the GPU's speed drift. =make bench-overhead=
  measures what the instrumentation adds.
- =make bench-app= starts each variant's executable on each task and measures it
  from outside: the time to its first presented frame, the frames, CPU and
  wakeups of ten seconds in which nothing happens (after it has drawn nothing
  for three seconds), the memory, and the size of the file raw and gzipped.
  =make bench-compile= compiles each variant in a new process with an empty
  cache. Results go to =results/= (version 2, see its README).

* Evidence
- The spikes. A saved executable works with AppKit and Metal once the layer is
  made again (the alien callables survive). =define-alien-callable= returns an
  HFA struct by value: a callable that returned a rect was called through a
  function pointer as C would call it and gave 1, 2, 3, 4, so the IME's
  rectangle can be returned from Lisp and no C shim is needed. (A callable that
  returns a double leaves the other three registers as they were, which is what
  returning an NSRect from one would have done.)
- Idle, the floor of the charter: in every one of the 21 apps (three variants,
  seven tasks), a window left alone for ten seconds drew no frame, used 0.0% CPU
  and made no wakeups (0.1 a second once). The check can fail: the timer,
  watched while it runs, drew 301 frames in 5 s at 5.5% CPU; once it stopped, 0
  and 0.0%.
- Startup, from starting the executable to its first presented frame, the median
  of five launches over the seven tasks: immediate 200 ms, retained 218,
  reactive 222 (a first launch after an image is made can be 3 to 4 times
  slower, and is the one that sets the top of the ranges in the records).
- Memory, the peak resident size seen and the heap in use: immediate 133 MB and
  52 MB, retained 142 to 143 MB and 53 to 54 MB, reactive 137 to 145 MB and 55
  to 56 MB. The resident size after the idle window is not comparable (70 to 145
  MB): the system compresses the memory of a window that is not used. The
  executables are 45.6 (retained), 45.7 and 45.9 MB (immediate), 12.9 to 13.0 MB
  gzipped: the Lisp is nearly all of it.
- Compile, from an empty cache: the core 3.2 s; the retained library 0.6 s, the
  reactive library 0.17 s more, the immediate library 0.27 s more; a variant's
  seven apps 0.04 to 0.07 s; after touching one library file, 0.6 s (retained, a
  file at the start of its library), 0.15 s, 0.25 s; after touching an app, 0.04
  to 0.07 s.
- What the instrumentation costs: 40 to 230 ns a frame on the example scripts,
  from 0.6% of a cells-edit build to 13% of a counter build (0.7 us), and
  nothing that shows on the null variant through the host (313.6 us a frame off,
  309.9 on). The clock ticks in 41.7 ns steps, so a difference of one or two
  ticks is all that can be seen at that size. Running 5,000 frames with it on
  allocates nothing.
- The phases of the scripts counter-clicks, timer-reset and cells-edit, through
  the host (interleaved, median, microseconds), the build with its update,
  layout and paint, and the share of the build time that is in builds after the
  first:

  The immediate variant's time is in its update (describing the interface again,
  with the hashes that make layout and paint skippable): its layout and paint
  cost almost nothing when what was described is what was presented, and it is
  the only variant that builds more than once for an event (the builds after the
  first are 51 to 66% of its build time in the two scripts with clicks and
  typing, because a widget that took input asks for the interface to be
  described again). The same build costs 3 to 7 times as much in a host run as
  in the hot loop of the headless run (the timer's retained build is 1.1 us
  there and 7.5 us here): in a window the CPU has been waiting for the display
  between frames, and the headless numbers are the best case. All three variants
  draw the same list in these scripts, so the GPU's time is the same: 52 us for
  the counter, 85 us for the timer, 224 us for cells.

* Decision
Proposed, for Micah to accept or change:
- The instrumentation is switched on at run time, by a flag in the context, and
  not at build time as the charter says. One image can then measure its own
  overhead (above), and what is off costs one test of a flag. A build-time
  switch would be a second build of every variant to compare. If the numbers
  above are not wanted in a release, the flag stays off, or the macro can be
  made to expand to nothing.
- Executables are of a variant with all its tasks, saved after loading, with the
  Objective-C layer made again at start; the platform layer is kept saveable by
  the test that saves an image.
- The protocol for what an app costs a machine: executables, started from
  outside, first frame by the core clock, idle after three quiet seconds for ten
  seconds, with =top= and =ps=, the first launch kept apart. Host runs are
  interleaved by variant. The headless runs stay as the stable measure of the
  variant's own work; host-run numbers are read across variants within a run.

* Consequences
- Easier: a frame can be looked at in Perfetto; the cost of settling and of each
  of the variant's phases is a number; the idle floor is a check that can be run
  for every app; a saved executable is a thing the project can make.
- Harder: every ring record is twice as large; the platform layer's classes must
  be made with =define-runtime-class= and its startup state with
  =define-startup=, or an image will not start.
- Open: the host numbers are of an off-screen texture and not of a window (no
  vsync, no wait for a drawable), and the GPU's speed varies from run to run;
  the phases a variant defines are three; CI tracking is not done (it needs a
  runner with a GPU: Micah has an old Mac that is not arm64, which would need
  the Objective-C layer ported first, because struct arguments are passed as
  they are on arm64); startup is of a window that opens on a desktop that is
  already running, with a warm cache after the first launch.
