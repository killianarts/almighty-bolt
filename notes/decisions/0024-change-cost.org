#+TITLE: 0024 — Change cost and size: what five edits cost in each variant
#+DATE: 2026-10-26
- Status :: proposed

* Context
Step 2 of Phase 3: H2 says that immediate mode with a cache gives the smallest
change cost, because building, mutation and interaction code are in one place.
The charter's test is a set of scripted edits applied to each variant, measuring
the size of the diff, with the lines of code and the concepts of each app as the
size. =notes/change-cost.org= says what the five edits are and how they are
counted; it was written before any edit was made.

* What was done
- Each edit was a specification and an acceptance test that is the same for
  every variant (the driver runs it against each). The tests that describe the
  new behaviour failed in all three variants before the edit, and pass after it
  (a test that guards behaviour that should not change passes before and after).
  The shared parts (the model of a task, the tests) were changed once; each
  variant's own app was then changed, in an order that is rotated from one edit
  to the next.
- The size of a change is the lines of code added plus those removed, not
  counting blank lines, comment lines, or a change of white space
  (=scripts/diff-size.lisp=, itself checked on a commit of a comment, a blank
  line, a re-indented line and one line of code: it counts the one). The size of
  an app is its lines of code and the number of distinct symbols it imports from
  the variant's packages (=scripts/app-size.lisp=).
- The edits are kept as patches in =results/change-cost/=, against the commit
  they began at, and as branches =change-cost/e1= to =e5= in the working copy
  that made them, not merged into the apps.
- The edits: E1 an Email field in the CRUD form; E2 an optional return date in
  the flight booker; E3 an Undo button in the temperature converter; E4 the
  filter row of CRUD behind a switch (a choice of two options stands in for a
  checkbox, which no variant has); E5 the flight booker's controls in two
  columns.

* Evidence
Lines of code changed (added and removed) in each part of each edit:
  | Edit | Tests | Model | Retained | Reactive | Immediate |
  |------+-------+-------+----------+----------+-----------|
  | E1 field   |    40 |    18 |       20 |       20 |        24 |
  | E2 optional |    47 |    16 |        6 |        4 |         4 |
  | E3 undo    |    35 |     0 |       31 |       18 |        12 |
  | E4 switch  |    24 |     0 |       26 |       18 |        15 |
  | E5 columns |    28 |     0 |        9 |        9 |         9 |
  | sum        |   174 |    34 |       92 |       69 |        64 |
- Over the five edits the immediate variant's code changed least (64 lines
  against 69 reactive and 92 retained). It is the smallest in E3 and E4, tied
  with the reactive in E2 and with both in E5, and the largest in E1 (24 against
  20 and 20).
- The order in which the variants were done shows no sign of the first paying
  more: the first done was the least, or tied for the least, in four of the five
  edits (E1 retained 20, E2 reactive 4, E3 immediate 12, E5 reactive 9) and the
  most in E4 (retained, 26).
- The retained variant costs most where an edit needs what a widget held before
  a change (E3: 31 lines, with a copy of the last texts of both fields and the
  enabling of the button by hand) or needs a handle on something to show or hide
  it (E4). The reactive and immediate variants have the old values and the
  condition at hand.
- The size of the apps: 576, 547 and 475 lines of code in the retained, reactive
  and immediate variants, and 71, 74 and 53 concepts. The immediate variant is
  the smallest in six of the seven tasks (the retained is, in temperature, by
  two lines) and uses the fewest concepts in all seven.
- Findings about the variants that the edits turned up: the retained
  =lookup-target= gives the old rectangle of a control that is inside a hidden
  node; the immediate variant finds a control by a click outside the window, so
  that E4's tests passed in it before the window was made taller and failed in
  the other two; a required argument added to a model function is found by the
  callers when they run, not when they are compiled.

* Decision
Proposed: H2 stays OPEN, and is not decided by this. The results lean toward it
(the immediate variant changed least over the five edits and its apps are
smallest), but they are five small edits, written by one author who also wrote
the variants, and counted in lines; the largest gap is in one edit that needs
what a widget held before a change, which is a feature of the retained widgets
and not of retention as an approach (a retained text field could say what it
held). The method is kept as the protocol: a specification and a shared test
first, the shared change apart, variants in rotated order, patches kept, white
space and comments not counted. The five edits stay as the first set; more would
make the measure better.

* Consequences
- Easier: the cost of a change is a number that can be repeated: the patches are
  in the repository and the report prints the table.
- Open: the usability rubric is for Micah to score (=notes/usability-rubric.org=
  has the sheet and the facts); there is no checkbox widget in any variant, and
  E4 used a choice, which Micah can ask to have replaced by a checkbox (it would
  be library work in each variant, and a change-cost datum of its own); edits
  that change the architecture and not an app (a new widget, a new kind of
  event) are not in this set.
