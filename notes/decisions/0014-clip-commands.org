#+TITLE: 0014 — Clipping in the display list
#+DATE: 2026-10-26
- Status :: accepted (autonomous, unreviewed)

* Context
0013 left clipping out and drew partly visible rows by not drawing them. The circle
drawer showed that this does not hold: a circle near the edge of the canvas spills over
the buttons and the window, and a grid or a list needs to cut what is drawn at its edge
(and a scrolling area at any edge it has). Every variant needs the same thing, and where
the commands are drawn is the renderer's.

* Options
1. Keep drawing only what is fully inside, and cover the spill with rectangles in the
   colour of what is behind.
2. Cut the shapes on the CPU, in the variant, to the clip rectangle.
3. Two commands in the display list, one that starts a clip and one that ends it, which
   the renderer turns into scissor rectangles.

* Evidence
- Option 1 hides the buttons that sit next to the canvas as well, unless the covers are
  as clever as the clip; option 2 cannot cut a rounded rectangle or a glyph to a
  straight edge and give the same shape.
- The renderer draws every command as one instance of one instanced draw, in list order,
  which keeps the order things are painted in. A scissor rectangle is a piece of state
  of the encoder, so the instances between two changes of it are drawn by a separate
  draw call. Metal's =baseInstance= makes a call start in the middle of the buffer;
  =instance_id= includes it, which a test of a stretch drawn after a clip confirms (a
  green rectangle drawn in the third stretch comes out in the right place).
- With no clip in the list the renderer takes the path it had, one call, so an app that
  does not clip pays nothing: 16 renderer tests, with 5 new, and the unchanged ones,
  pass.

* Decision
Option 3. =push-clip= (x, y, width, height in points) clips what follows to that rectangle
and to every clip already on, and =push-unclip= ends the innermost. The renderer turns the
pairs into stretches of instances, each drawn with a scissor rectangle of pixels
(rounded outward), and does not draw a stretch whose clip is empty or outside the
target. At most 32 clips nest and 4,096 stretches make a frame, which are errors, and an
unmatched =push-unclip= is ignored. The two commands take places in the list, and
=append-display-list= moves a clip with the rest, so a cached node can hold one.
The retained variant's canvas clips what its app draws to its own area.

* Consequences
- Easier: the grid, the lists and the canvas keep what they draw inside themselves in
  every variant, and a node's cache holds its clip.
- Harder: a command in a list is not always a thing drawn, so anything that counts
  commands to count shapes (the tests' =shapes-in=, for one) skips these; the capacity of
  a list counts them.
- Gives up: clips that are not rectangles, and a stencil for rounded ones.
