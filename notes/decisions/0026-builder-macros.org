#+TITLE: 0026 — Builder macros: how much of the size gap is syntax
#+DATE: 2026-10-26
- Status :: accepted (Micah, 2026-10-27), option (a)
- Merged :: the layer and the port; the signal-model files stay on
  the branch =builder-macros=

* Context
0024 found the immediate variant's apps the smallest (475 lines against 547
reactive and 576 retained for the seven 7GUIs tasks; 660, 791 and 816 with the
three stress apps). Micah saw that they also look nearly usable as they are, and
asked whether that is because the model is shaped for immediate mode or because
the other two must wire a change to the widgets it changes; and said the
retained and reactive variants looked as if they needed a macro API. The
variants differ in syntax as well as in approach: the immediate containers are
macros whose body is the children, and the others are functions with the
children first and the options after, which for a computed row needs =(apply
#'row (append (loop ...) (list :gap ...)))=. The two experiments take the syntax
away as an explanation. =notes/builder-macros.org= was committed with the method
and what I expected before any macro was written.

* What was done
- A builder package in each variant (one file,
  =variants/cl-retained/src/builder.lisp= and
  =variants/cl-reactive/src/builder.lisp=): =row=, =column=, =stack=, =padding=,
  =panel= and =center= as macros with the immediate variant's syntax, =(row
  (:gap 10) child ...)=, and for reactive =(component () body ...)=. A body
  form's value is a child; a list is spliced in; NIL is left out. The macros
  expand into functions that take the children as one list (=flex-box-of=,
  =stack-of=, =flex-view-of=), which the old constructors now call, so no
  existing caller changed and no copy of the children is made. 17 tests.
- The ten counted apps of both variants were rewritten with them, and nothing
  else in them. =scripts/frame-hashes.lisp= (the hash of each app's frame after
  a battery of events, in the acceptance driver) gives the same 168 lines before
  and after, and =make test= passes.
- A reactive dashboard whose model is signals
  (=apps/stress/dashboard/signal-model.lisp=, =reactive-signals.lisp=; a system
  of its own, not in =make test=, that replaces the reactive dashboard when
  loaded): the same frames, the dashboard's acceptance tests pass.
- Two new scripts: =scripts/app-tokens.lisp=, the size of an app in tokens,
  which does not change when a child is put on a line of its own, and
  =scripts/frame-bytes.lisp=, the bytes and time of a frame of an animated app
  in the driver.

* Evidence
Lines of code and tokens of the apps, before the port (4cb93b8) and after; the
immediate variant was not changed:
  |                    | retained     | reactive     | immediate |
  |--------------------+--------------+--------------+-----------|
  | lines, 7GUIs       | 576 -> 590   | 547 -> 550   | 475       |
  | lines, stress      | 240 -> 241   | 244 -> 241   | 185       |
  | lines, all ten     | 816 -> 831   | 791 -> 791   | 660       |
  | tokens, whole file | 2473 -> 2487 | 2426 -> 2427 | 1905      |
  | tokens, body       | 2059 -> 2053 | 2039 -> 2020 | 1568      |
- The macros close 1.2 % of retained's gap to immediate and 4.0 % of reactive's,
  in tokens of the body (what an app is but its package form). In lines they
  widen retained's gap by 10 % and leave reactive's alone. Ten lines in each
  variant are the cost of the macros being in a package of their own (one more
  IMPORT-FROM clause in each app); without them reactive's gap is 8 % smaller.
  The dashboards gain three lines each, where I expected ten. The syntax of the
  tree is at most 4 % of the gap of the body. The concepts (97, 102 and 73) do
  not change.
- A frame conses the same bytes and takes the same time before and after, in the
  two apps that animate: the dashboard 623, 9435 and 3537 bytes (retained,
  reactive, immediate) and the timer 33, 98 and 590.
- The signal model: the app is 69 lines and 149 body tokens against 80 and 193,
  the model file is 42 lines and 86 tokens, so together they are larger. A frame
  conses 10058 bytes against 9435 (+6.6 %) and takes 74.2 us against 70.1 (+5.8
  %). It removes the loop that copies the model into 36 signals and moves its
  work into the model.
Where the rest of the gap is, in tokens of the whole file, after the port
(retained, reactive): the gap is 582 and 522; the package form is 97 and 70 of
it (an app imports from three to five packages of the retained variant, four of
the reactive and two of the immediate); the callbacks' signatures are 77 and 33
(a handler that must say it ignores the canvas or the data it is given: 26 and
13 declarations in the ten apps against 4); and 408 and 419, 70 % and 80 %, are
the rest, which I did not split.
- By my reading the rest is the code that keeps the widgets or the signals in
  step with the model (in the dashboard: the handles and the loop that writes
  them; the signals, the components that read them and the ticker that writes
  them) and the event model (a named handler given to a widget, against =(when
  (button ...))= where the widget is described). The immediate dashboard has no
  code of the first kind, because the description reads the model; the library
  does the work of finding what changed in every frame. What that costs in time
  is small: the dashboard's build in the driver is 43.4, 70.1 and 74.6 us
  (retained, reactive, immediate), so retained is clearly cheaper and the other
  two are close.

* Decision
Proposed: H2 stays OPEN. What this adds is that the size gap of 0024 is not the
syntax of the tree (at most 4 % of the body's gap). A measurable part is how the
libraries are laid out, which is not about approach: the package form (13 to 17
% of the gap of the whole file) and the signatures of callbacks (7 to 16 % of
the gap of the body). Most of it, 70 to 80 %, I take by reading to be the code
that keeps the state in step with the widgets and the event model, which is what
H2 supposed; I did not measure it apart from the rest. The answer to the
question that began this: the models are not shaped for the immediate variant;
the shared editor controller returns whether it changed something, which the
immediate variant discards, and a signal cannot see a model that changes in
place, which the reactive variant has to work around.

Proposed, for Micah to choose (the work is on the branch =builder-macros=, which
starts at 4cb93b8, not merged and not pushed):
- (a) Merge the builder layer and the port. The three variants' trees then read
  the same, which is what whoever scores the usability rubric should be
  comparing. The costs are that every size quoted in 0024, 0025 and the rubric's
  facts sheet (=notes/usability-rubric.org=) changes (retained +15 lines,
  reactive 0), so they would be quoted before and after; and that the patches of
  E1 to E5 (=results/change-cost/=) no longer apply to the apps, so a sixth edit
  would not be like for like unless the five were made again. I recommend this.
- (b) Merge the layer and its tests, and leave the apps of record as they are.
  Nothing that was measured changes; new apps can use the macros, and the rubric
  compares function calls with macros.
- (c) Do not merge. The branch keeps the evidence.
Either way the signal-model system stays on the branch as the experiment's
evidence; it is not a candidate for the apps of record.

* Consequences
- Easier: the next comparison of variants can say that the syntax is not what
  differs. The builder macros are additive and cost nothing that was measured.
- Harder: more to keep in step. The builder packages follow the constructors (a
  new container needs a macro), and an app that takes its containers from them
  has one more import.
- Open: macros in which a widget that is called in the body puts itself in, as
  in the immediate variant (so that =dotimes= works and nothing is built first
  and assembled last), are not tried. The retained apps build their widgets
  first because they need handles to change them later, so the saving would be
  the assembly forms, a line each. The 620 bytes a frame that 36 computed values
  cost was not looked into. The rubric is Micah's to score, and its facts sheet
  is to be changed if (a) is chosen.
