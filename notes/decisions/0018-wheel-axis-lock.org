#+TITLE: 0018 — A trackpad gesture goes along one axis
#+DATE: 2026-10-26
- Status :: accepted (made in answer to Micah's report on 2026-10-26; record not yet reviewed)

* Context
On the cells grid Micah's trackpad scrolled diagonally. A swipe meant to go
across carried about a fifth of its length down (=dx -26, dy -5= an event), and
one meant to go down carried a little across. The grid read both. A flick was
clean (=dy 0=), a calm swipe was not. 0017 made the unit right, and said nothing
about the axes.

* Options
1. The cells app drops the smaller axis of each event.
2. The core offers a small state machine that locks a gesture to its main axis,
   and the variants use it where wheel events come in, so that every view that
   scrolls gets it.
3. The same, using the platform's gesture phases (=phase= and =momentumPhase= of
   the NSEvent) to know when a gesture begins and ends.

* Evidence
- Option 1 drops the drift of a single event, which is not the drift of a
  gesture: the events of Micah's calm swipe had a ratio that varied from 1 to
  0.15, and the first ones were nearly diagonal (=-3, -3=). Choosing for each
  event would flicker.
- Option 3 needs two new fields on the wheel event, in every platform's
  translation, and the replay format. The events already carry timestamps, from
  which a pause can be seen.
- Option 2 is deterministic from the events, so a replay locks as the original
  did.
- Micah's logs: events 8 to 16 ms apart during a gesture, momentum included,
  with no pause of more than a few frames until it stops.
- A 45 degree movement has no main axis. It is given to down, as a tie.
- Tested: the swipes from Micah's log, a pause ending a gesture, the axis
  staying through momentum, a wheel's lines passing through untouched, and, in
  all three variants, the grid moving across only for a swipe across with drift,
  and down only for a swipe down with drift. With the lock taken out, those six
  tests fail.

* Decision
Option 2. =lock-wheel= (=core/src/wheel-lock.lisp=) takes a lock and a wheel
event and returns the scroll in lines as =wheel-event-lines= does. For a
trackpad's precise events it adds up the points moved along each axis from the
start of the gesture, locks the gesture to the larger axis as soon as it has 4
points, uses the one that has gone further until then, and gives the other axis
as zero. A gesture is the events that follow each other with no pause of 150 ms.
A wheel's events, in lines, are not touched. The retained variant (and so the
reactive) keeps a lock in the variant, and the immediate one in its input.

* Consequences
- Easier: a swipe goes one way in every scrolling view, and a view that only
  scrolls down cannot be nudged sideways by drift, nor the grid up and down.
- Harder: no gesture is diagonal, in a view that could use it (a map). Changing
  direction without lifting the fingers or pausing is ignored on the other axis.
  Two gestures less than 150 ms apart (a flick down and at once a swipe across)
  share the first one's axis; with the phases that could not happen.
- Not checked: how 4 points and 150 ms feel under a finger; Micah's trackpad is
  the test.
