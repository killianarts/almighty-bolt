#+DATE: 2026-10-27

* Context
0024 made E4 (hide the CRUD's filter row behind a switch) with a choice of two
options, because no variant had a checkbox, and said a real one would be library
work in each variant and a change-cost datum of its own. Micah, 2026-10-27: E4
should use a real checkbox. The method, the interface of the widget, the base
and what I expected were committed before any code (=notes/change-cost.org=,
61bb500).

* What was done
- A checkbox in each variant, one commit each, with tests: retained 8671821,
  reactive 70b01d9, immediate b39b437. A box with a label; a click on the box or
  the label that ends over it, or the space bar with the keyboard, changes it;
  Tab reaches it, a ring shows the keyboard, a disabled one takes nothing.
  Retained: =(checkbox label :id :checked :on-change)= and =checkbox-checked=.
  Reactive: =(checkbox label :id :checked :on-change)=, the app owning the state
  and a refused change undone. Immediate: =(checkbox id label checked)=
  returning =(values checked changed)=. The mark is a filled square in the box,
  not a tick. The behaviour was broken on purpose in six places (the space bar,
  a release outside, the controlled handler, the state written back, a press
  outside, the mark) and a test failed each time; nothing used the widgets, and
  the 168 frame hashes of the ten apps were the same.
- E4 on the branch =change-cost/e4-checkbox= (3c7eb8a, from b39b437), not
  merged, as the other edits are not: the four acceptance tests first (3 failed
  and 9 errored in the three variants), then the apps in the order immediate,
  reactive, retained; the window 40 points taller. Patches in
  =results/change-cost/e4-checkbox/=; =results/20261027-e4-checkbox.txt= has the
  tables.

* Evidence
Lines of code (=scripts/diff-size.lisp=), the library of each variant and then
the edit:
  |           | library | its tests | the edit | predicted (library, edit) |
  |-----------+---------+-----------+----------+---------------------------|

The shared tests are 24 lines (predicted 18 to 24).
- Every number fell in the range I wrote down but one: the reactive library is
  14, one under. None of the three conditions that I wrote down as making the
  expectation wrong held: the reactive library is not larger than the immediate
  one, the immediate edit is not larger than the retained one, and no edit is
  over 26 lines.
- The edit is 12 lines in the reactive and immediate variants and 22 in the
  retained: the retained one needs a handle on the row to hide it (a variable
  for the row and one for the checkbox, =node-visible=, and the list rewritten
  when it changes), and the other two say =(when on ...)= where the row is.
- The reactive checkbox is small because the retained node does the work: a
  view, an element and two property tables. The retained and immediate ones are
  the same size, 54 and 53, for different work: a node with layout, painting,
  pointer and keys; and a kind of box with layout, painting and events.

* Decision
Proposed: H2 stays OPEN. The checkboxes stay in the libraries (they are product
code and are on phase-2). What the edit shows is one more edit of the 7GUIs kind
in which the immediate and reactive variants cost the same and the retained one
costs about twice as much, which is what the five edits of 0024 leaned towards.

Proposed, for Micah to choose (the edit is on the branch
=change-cost/e4-checkbox=, local and not pushed):
- (a) Keep the edit on the branch as evidence, as the other five are. I
  recommend this.
- (b) Merge it into the three CRUD apps, so that the apps of record have the
  checkbox. This changes their sizes again (every size quoted in 0024, 0025 and
  0026 would be quoted before and after) and the window of the CRUD app.
- (c) Make E4 with the choice again on this base, so that the effect of a real
  checkbox can be told from the effect of the macro port: the numbers above are
  not like for like with 0024's table, because the retained apps are 15 lines
  longer since 0026.

* Consequences
- Easier: every variant has a checkbox, with the same behaviour, and later apps
  can use it; a sixth edit has a base with a widget in it.
- Harder: three more widgets to keep, 54, 14 and 53 lines, and their tests, 66,
  41 and 67; the painting of the mark is the same three times.
- Open: a tick instead of a filled square; whether the checkbox takes the
  keyboard on a click (the three do not, as the choice does not); one author
  wrote the variants, the widgets and the edit.
